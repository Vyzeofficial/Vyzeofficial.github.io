Most laptop listings are written to sell you something. We read them for you.

The words on a listing and the hardware in the box often don't match. We are building tools to help users know what they are buying. There is no account to make, and we earn nothing from the laptops we link to.

Example listing
#1 Best Seller

NOVA Pro 17 Newest Gaming Laptop, 12th Quad-Core Processor, 16GB RAM, 512GB SSD, 17.3" Display, Up to 18 Hours Battery

$329.00List price $1,499.00
  1. Gaming Laptop. No graphics card is named. Games need one.
  2. 12th Quad-Core Processor. Which chip? The listing never says.
  3. Up to 18 Hours. The best result in a lab test, not typical use.
  4. List price $1,499.00. A price the laptop may never have sold at.
  5. #1 Best Seller. A sales rank inside a tiny category, not a quality score.

We made this listing up. Every move in it comes from real ones on the Evidence page.

What "up to" means in practice

A battery claim is the best result from a manufacturer's test. HP lists its OmniBook 5 at up to 34 hours. Tom's Guide ran its own test and got 16.
